1. Node-link visualization

Drag stations to untangle connections. Hover or keyboard-focus a station to highlight its neighbors; hover a route for its travel time. Labels use station IDs (s7 = Station 7).

Node-link design

District colors distinguish administrative groups; circle area grows with passenger volume to emphasize busy stations. Borders distinguish local (thin), transfer (thick), and terminal (dashed) stations. Route color identifies service; thicker links mean longer travel, emphasizing time cost. Dragging separates overlaps, highlighting reveals neighbors, and tooltips give exact values. Layout position is not geographic.

2. Adjacency matrix

Rows and columns use the same station order. Colored cells show direct connections; darker cells mean longer travel (2–16 minutes). Pale cells mean no connection. Each undirected route appears twice.

Matrix design

Both axes list stations, initially grouped by district with colored labels. Cell color preserves route type and opacity increases with travel time. Empty cells remain pale; symmetry reflects undirected travel. Ordering exposes district blocks or the numbered backbone. Label tooltips preserve passenger volume and station type without crowding cells.
